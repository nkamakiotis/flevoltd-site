01 · The approach

Improvement starts where the last incident ended.

A risk assessment that never meets a real incident is an opinion. Incident analysis tells you how losses actually happen; risk assessment uses it to stop the next one.

  1. Q1How can you improve without analysing what already went wrong?Industry learned this the hard way: no investigation, no lessons, the same accident again. Wildfires and floods are no different. They are incidents, and they can be analysed like incidents.
  2. Q2How do you calibrate a risk assessment if its weights never meet an actual loss?Every factor, weight and threshold should trace back to how losses actually happened. That is what turns a checklist into a risk assessment.
  3. Q3Who closes the loop?The investigator who reconstructs the incident and the analyst who scores the next risk should work from the same evidence. Here they are the same person, and the same platform.

08 · About

Engineer, investigator, risk analyst.

Mechanical engineer (Aristotle University of Thessaloniki) with postgraduate studies in water resources (National Technical University of Athens). Fifteen years running safety, security and environmental management for port, oil storage and airport operators in Cyprus, including DP World and VTTI. ISO 45001 Lead Auditor, Port Facility Security Officer, NEBOSH Oil & Gas.

In July 2018, watching the Mati disaster unfold, I asked why wildfires are not analysed the way industry analyses its incidents. The reconstructions that followed now run alongside my safety work, and feed the products built on them.

Same cycle, same discipline: analyse what happened, turn it into risk factors, reduce the risk before the next incident.
