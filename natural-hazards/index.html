2024

Varnavas, East Attica

The two most violent fire heads of the first 24 hours, in ten-minute steps, as the fire reached the north-eastern suburbs of Athens. The flight tracks of every firefighting aircraft were analysed: drops in speed and height mark water drops and add time-stamped evidence of where the fronts were. Within the perimeter, two communities with very different layouts were compared side by side. Followed by a scientific mission at the National Observatory of Athens on wildland–urban interface fires.

2026

Oraiokastro, Thessaloniki

A night-time fire on 4–5 July 2026 that burned into an industrial zone and residential streets on the edge of the city. The reconstruction maps the fire against every factory, warehouse and home in its path, including combustibles stored outdoors and illegal dump sites that standard vegetation maps do not show.

It looks like a simulation. Every line is evidence.

A simulation starts from equations and predicts where a fire could go. This method starts from records of where the fire actually was, and at what time. Physics is used only to connect those records, never to replace them.

  1. Collect. Videos and photos posted by residents, news footage, interviews with responders and witnesses, webcam recordings, satellite images and firefighting aircraft tracks.
  2. Locate and time every item. Where it was filmed from and where the front is in the frame, matched against pre-fire imagery; when it was filmed, from the post itself, other footage, the sun's position or shadow lengths.
  3. Fix the milestones. Each located and timed observation becomes a fire-front line, starting with the ignition point, which sets the clock for everything else.
  4. Cross-check them. A milestone that the evidence, wind, slope and fuel cannot support is re-examined or rejected.
  5. Fill the gaps. Ten-minute curves are drawn between milestones, inside the burned area, spaced by recorded wind, slope and gullies, vegetation, roads and man-made fuel.
  6. Animate. The curves are played in sequence on satellite imagery or 3D terrain.

From incident analysis to risk assessment

Reconstruction and risk assessment are two stages of the same cycle, as in industrial safety management. Every incident investigated shows how losses actually happen; that knowledge is fed back to assess the next risk more accurately and reduce it before the next fire.

  1. IncidentFire reconstructed from evidence
  2. Loss mechanismsWhat made buildings burn and fronts accelerate
  3. Risk factorsWeighted at country, district, community and property level
  4. Risk reducedUnderwriting conditions, mitigation and in-season alerts

PYRODOTUS is where that cycle runs before the incident. It scores the long-term wildfire risk of a property and turns it into an underwriting decision, carrying the lessons of reconstructed fires. Built for Cyprus first and endorsed by the Cyprus Department of Forests.

  • Long-term riskA composite score from 74 property factors, from site and surroundings to construction and maintenance, with a confidence index and a printable risk action report.
  • Daily alert systemA seven-day fire weather outlook for each insured location, computed with the Canadian Fire Weather Index chain.
  • Community mapSettlement-level risk for portfolio accumulation.
